# DPI-01 — DPI and GovTech Architecture

> Status: Draft | Version: 1.0.0 | Owner: DPI Architecture Lead

## Purpose
Design modular, interoperable digital public infrastructure that can support public services without creating unnecessary vendor or platform dependencies.

## Scope
Digital identity, registries, payments, data exchange, consent, messaging, service delivery, digital signatures, public-service platforms, and shared infrastructure.

## Required inputs
Service and jurisdiction context; legal mandate; institutional owners; user journeys; system inventory; data-sharing basis; interoperability requirements; threat model; operating and funding model.

## Workflow
1. Define the public problem and service outcome before selecting a component.
2. Map institutions, authoritative data sources, service providers, users, and accountability.
3. Classify candidate digital public goods correctly: software, data, AI systems/models, open content, standards, or other digital resources.
4. Assess candidate components for functional fit, maturity, governance, license, security, localization, maintainability, community health, and total cost.
5. Define APIs, identifiers, data schemas, consent, authentication, audit, and interoperability profiles.
6. Design phased adoption and coexistence with legacy systems.
7. Define service ownership, funding, support, incident handling, and exit/replace strategy.
8. Validate with legal, security, privacy, accessibility, and operational stakeholders.

## Deliverables
DPI capability map; component assessment; interoperability profile; integration architecture; data-sharing map; adoption roadmap; governance and operating model.

## Quality gates
No component is labeled a production-ready DPI building block without verified evidence. DPGA registry counts must be recorded with retrieval date and classification; registry membership is not proof of local suitability or deployment readiness.

## Controls
No real citizen data in prototypes. No automated high-impact decisions. No central data exchange without lawful basis, accountable owners, security controls, and documented purpose limitation.

## Evaluation
Measure reuse, interoperability, service accessibility, lifecycle cost, operational sustainability, and reduction of duplicated infrastructure.
